STR rules by town

  • Statewide — RI 5% whole-home STR tax: confirmed in effect since January 1, 2026 and unchanged as of today. Applies to whole-home short-term rentals (houses, condos, mobile homes) on top of existing state sales tax and the state hotel tax, which itself rose from 1% to 2% — roughly a 14% combined lodging tax load. Liability is keyed to date of occupancy, not date of booking. (RI Division of Taxation FAQ, RI REALTORS)
  • Narragansett RI (includes Bonnet Shores RI) — the 2024 ordinance sets a 7-night minimum, bans STR use for weddings/parties, and caps permits (1,100 in 2024 stepping down to 900 by 2026). Status today: still under preliminary injunction. Judge Sarah Taft-Carter granted the landlord group Narragansett 2100 a TRO and preliminary injunction in October 2024, and a follow-up ruling again went against the town. No trial date or final ruling was found this run — the cap is not currently being enforced, but this is unsettled and reversible, not repealed. (Boston Globe, GoLocalProv)
  • Warwick RI — the highest-risk town on this list for a buy-and-rent model. City officials have stated current zoning technically bans all short-term rentals citywide, enforced complaint-driven via cease-and-desist letters. The 2024–2025 ordinance rewrite would permit STRs only where owner-occupied, banning non-owner-occupied units and ADUs outright in residential zones — precisely the model this section screens for — with later reporting describing 2026 rules further restricting R-6/R-10 zones, which cover Oakland Beach and Gaspee. AirDNA nonetheless shows 124 active Warwick listings in 2026 (up 4.2% year over year), implying de facto tolerance. Verify the adopted ordinance text and zone map with Warwick's Building/Planning Department before buying here — do not rely on secondary summaries. (reporting, 2026 guide)
  • South Kingstown RI (Wakefield RI / Peace Dale RI / Matunuck RI) — registration required for all rentals under 30 days with the Building Official; $100 fee, $300 late fee, fines to $500 for non-compliance; 2026 filing deadline extended to March 31, 2026. No outright ban — the friendliest regime among the focus towns. (Town of South Kingstown)
  • Jamestown RI — total units capped (87 registered per recent reporting). Operators need an RI DBR registration certificate, a Town Council–approved Jamestown STR license, and a building inspection certificate. Whether the 87-unit cap is currently full or waitlisted could not be confirmed this run — a material unknown for both Jamestown listings above. (ordinance summary)
  • North Kingstown RI — a registry has been under discussion/development; 54 units appear on the state registry per the most recent reporting found. No confirmed outright ban. (The Independent)
