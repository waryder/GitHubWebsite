STR Rules by Town

  • Narragansett RI — 2024 permit-cap ordinance, re-checked 2026-08-20. Case is Narragansett 2100, Inc. et al. v. Town of Narragansett, Washington County Superior Court No. WC-2024-0372. Ordinance Chapter 1123 would cap STR permits on a declining schedule (1,100 in 2024 down to 900 by September 2026) and ban rentals under 7 consecutive nights. Judge Sarah Taft-Carter granted a TRO and preliminary injunction in fall 2024, finding plaintiffs likely to succeed because the advertising ban on sub-7-night stays likely conflicts with state law barring towns from prohibiting owners from listing on hosting platforms. The town has lost subsequent rounds and repeatedly delayed implementation. No 2026 merits ruling was found this run — the most recent accessible coverage runs through roughly January 2025. Treat the ordinance as still enjoined and unenforceable for planning purposes, but re-verify with the Town Clerk or the docket before closing on any Narragansett unit. Sources: WPRI · GoLocalProv · Ordinance text
  • Statewide Rhode Island, effective 2026-01-01. Local hotel tax rose from 1% to 2%. A new 5% whole-home STR tax applies to any residential dwelling rented in its entirety, on top of the existing 7% state sales tax and 5% state hotel tax framework. Combined lodging tax on whole-home STRs reaches up to 14%, applied by occupancy date (not booking date). Build this into any weekly-rate pro forma. Sources: Sales Tax Institute · RI Division of Taxation · Avalara
  • South Kingstown, North Kingstown, Warwick and Jamestown RI — general STR ordinance text was not independently pulled this run beyond the Carpenter's-beach and Narragansett findings above. Treat these towns' STR rules as unconfirmed and needing follow-up.
