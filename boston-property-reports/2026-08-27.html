Short-term-rental rules by town

  • Narragansett RI — permit cap still enjoined: the 2024 ordinance (7-night minimum stay, permit cap stepping 1,100 → 1,000 → 900 by 2026) remains under the preliminary injunction issued in October 2024 by Superior Court Judge Sarah Taft-Carter in Narragansett 2100 v. Town of Narragansett, and is unenforced as of the latest reporting. Town Solicitor James Callaghan has indicated the town is likely to re-draft the rules into the zoning ordinance as a more legally defensible path. Litigation unresolved. (Independent RI, Narragansett Times, WPRI)
  • RI statewide 5% whole-home STR tax — confirmed in effect: took effect 2026-01-01 on whole-dwelling short-term rentals, distinct from the 5% hotel tax on room rentals; combined with the local hotel tax doubling from 1% to 2%, the total lodging tax load runs up to 14%. (RI Division of Taxation FAQ, Sales Tax Institute, What’sUpNewp)
  • North Kingstown RI: no dedicated town-wide STR ordinance or registry found — governed through general zoning and building rules. Confirm homestead-exemption impact before buying. (Independent RI)
  • South Kingstown RI: Rental Registry under Town Code §9-200 to §9-210 — $100/yr registration, $300 late fee, fines up to $500. Not a cap or a ban. (southkingstownri.gov)
  • Jamestown RI: registration required since August 2023 — $350/yr fee, building and fire inspections, 2 occupants per bedroom cap, local contact reachable within 2 hours; council may deny “for just cause” and reserves the right to cap total permits. (Jamestown Press)
  • Warwick RI: STRs permitted in residential and mixed-use zones (not commercial or industrial) since February 2023, with safety and record-keeping requirements; affordable-designated units and repeat-violation units are barred from STR use. (warwickri.gov)
