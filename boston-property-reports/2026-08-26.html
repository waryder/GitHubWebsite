Short-term-rental rules by town

  • Narragansett RI — the 2024 ordinance requires permits, imposes a 7-night minimum and caps permits (1,100 in 2024 → 1,000 in 2025 → 900 in 2026, town discretion thereafter). Current status verified this run: the ordinance remains unenforceable. Superior Court Judge Sarah Taft-Carter granted a TRO/preliminary injunction in October 2024 in the Narragansett 2100 litigation, and the most recent reporting located shows the stay still in place with the Town Council and Solicitor now weighing a re-draft as a zoning ordinance rather than a business ordinance, to survive challenge — no vote taken. Plan on the cap being inactive today, but do not assume caps are permanently gone. Boston Globe · The Independent · Narragansett Times (zoning pivot) · GoLocalProv
  • South Kingstown RI — annual short-term residential rental registration required; $100 fee, $300 late fee, fines to $500 (Town Code Ch. 9 §§9-200–9-210). Active and enforced. Town of South Kingstown
  • North Kingstown RI (incl. Wickford RI, Saunderstown RI) — no dedicated townwide STR registry page; handled through zoning/building/assessor. Third-party data shows a high compliance signal (~97% of active listings carry registration evidence), implying active informal enforcement. Confirm with Building/Zoning before purchase. Municode · South County STR overview
  • Jamestown RI — an ordinance governs a limited pool (~87 STR units cited); details not fully verified this run. Check the town code directly before any purchase.
  • Warwick RI — no dedicated STR ordinance or registry page located; handled case-by-case through zoning. Confirm before purchase — this matters, because the Warwick shore supplies most of this run's affordable Section 2 inventory.
  • Statewide RI baseline — DBR registration is required before listing on any hosting platform, the registration number and expiration must appear on the listing, and accessory dwelling units may not be used for short-term rental under any circumstance (R.I. Gen. Laws §45-24-73). RI DBR FAQ
  • Statewide 5% whole-home STR tax — re-confirmed in effect. Effective 2026-01-01, a 5% Whole Home Short-Term Rental Tax applies to entire residential dwellings (houses, condos, mobile homes) rented for 30 consecutive days or fewer, assessed on date of occupancy rather than booking date. It stacks with the existing 7% sales tax and a local hotel tax rising from 1% to 2% — a combined stay-tax burden of up to 14%. RI Division of Taxation FAQ · Sales Tax Institute
