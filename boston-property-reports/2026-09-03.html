STR rules by town

  • Warren RI — REVERSAL, and the headline of this run. Carried for two consecutive runs as “cap unconfirmed past first reading,” the ordinance now appears to have been adopted at second/final reading around 2023-02-21: a cap of 125 STR units townwide, a 50%-of-units cap for buildings of 2–5 units, a $100/unit annual registration fee and a parking requirement. This is not a technicality — the 50%-per-building rule bears directly on all four Warren RI duplexes in Section 3, and the townwide cap means availability, not just eligibility, is the question. Confidence caveat: EastBayRI's article body would not render on direct fetch, so this rests on the outlet's search-indexed headline and text plus corroborating coverage rather than a full primary fetch. Verify with Warren Town Hall before relying on it, including whether the 125-unit cap is currently full. Sources: EastBayRI (adopted), EastBayRI (proposal).
  • Narragansett RI — still enjoined, still unresolved. The 2024 permit-cap ordinance (7-night minimum, declining annual cap toward 900 by September 2026, event bans) remains under the preliminary injunction issued by Superior Court Judge Sarah Taft-Carter in October 2024 in Narragansett 2100 v. Town of Narragansett. No 2026 ruling or settlement surfaced this run; the most recent concrete town communication found still dates to 2025-01-31, with the town registering STRs under the pre-ordinance sections 14-518–14-520 instead. Separately, the proposed duplex/two-family STR ban was never adopted — floored April 2024, revived as a broader duplex-zoning proposal in 2025, then withdrawn by the Town Council in May 2025 after resident opposition. Sources: WPRI, Town of Narragansett via Patch.
  • Warwick RI — contradiction persists for a second run. The City's own 2023-10-11 memo to the state STR legislative commission, fetched directly as a primary source, states STRs were added to the Warwick zoning use table as a permitted use in all residential and Village (mixed-use) zones, listing only categorical prohibitions (accessory units, affordable-designated units, code-violation properties, wedding-event properties) — and no R-6/R-10 carve-out whatsoever. Several 2026 syndicated sites still assert an R-6/R-10 ban. The current Municode Section 510 text could not be pulled to settle it. Treat the city's own memo as authoritative pending direct verification; this matters because five of the seven Section 2 candidates are in Warwick RI. Sources: City of Warwick memo (primary), BNBCalc (conflicting secondary).
  • North Kingstown RI — confirmed: no adopted local STR ordinance. A registry has been discussed but never codified; only statewide rules apply (RI DBR registration plus the new 5% tax). This is materially favorable, and it is where the closest-to-station Section 2 candidate sits. Confirm with Building/Zoning before purchase, as this can change. Sources: RI Realtors, Town of North Kingstown.
  • South Kingstown RI — two distinct ordinances, re-confirmed separate. (1) The Short-Term Residential Rental Registry, adopted 2024-04-08, Town Code Ch. 9 §§9-200–9-210: annual registration by December 31, $100 fee / $300 late fee, posting requirement, fines to $500. (2) A separate zoning track governing accessory apartments/ADUs (Zoning Ordinance §503.2C and §907, special use permit required) under which state law prohibits ADUs from being used as short-term rentals at all, regardless of the registry. Sources: Town registry notice, ADU regulations.
  • RI statewide 5% whole-home STR tax — confirmed from primary source. Effective for occupancy dates on or after 2026-01-01, a 5% tax applies to whole-home short-term rentals under 30 days, and the local hotel tax simultaneously rises from 1% to 2%. The tax keys to date of occupancy, not date of booking. Sources: RI Division of Taxation notice, RI Division of Taxation FAQ.
  • Barrington RI — no dedicated town STR ordinance surfaced this run. Treat as unverified rather than permissive, and confirm with the town.
