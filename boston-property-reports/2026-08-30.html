STR rules by town

  • Narragansett RI — permit cap, re-checked. The May 2024 ordinance (Chapter 1123) would cap STR permits at 1,100, stepping to 900 by September 2026, and ban rentals under 7 nights. It has been under injunction since October 2024, when Superior Court Judge Sarah Taft-Carter granted a TRO and preliminary injunction after landlord group Narragansett 2100 sued under the Home Rule Amendment. No final ruling found. The most recent confirmed status is a 2025-12-19 town council meeting at which Town Solicitor James Callaghan reported the ordinance had been non-enforceable for over a year and the town was weighing scrapping the business-ordinance approach in favor of re-enacting the rules as a zoning ordinance, considered more defensible. No votes were taken; no 2026 coverage updating this was found. The underlying registration/rental rules remain separately in force. Sources: Boston Globe, WPRI, RI Central (2025-12-19).
  • Warwick RI — R-6/R-10 zones. Confirmed from secondary sources only this run. The base STR ordinance (No. O-23-4) requires Building Department registration; the code was recodified under Ordinance No. O-25-11, adopted 2025-07-08. Secondary sources report that current Warwick zoning bars new short-term rentals in the R-6 and R-10 residential zones — which cover much of Oakland Beach and Gaspee — unless the owner occupies the property. The primary ordinance PDF and the Municode zoning text both returned blank on fetch this run, so zone boundaries and effective date could not be verified from a primary legal source. Verify with Warwick Planning before buying in Oakland Beach. Sources: City of Warwick; secondary Jaken Finance Group.
  • South Kingstown RI — rental registration. A townwide Residential Rental Registration ordinance was adopted 2025-10-14, building on an April 2024 STR registry. Fee $100, late fee $300, fines to $500. The original 2025-12-31 filing deadline was extended to 2026-03-31. Housing Authority units and deed-restricted affordable units are fee-exempt. Sources: Town of South Kingstown, The Independent.
  • Rhode Island statewide — 5% whole-home STR tax: confirmed, in effect. Effective 2026-01-01, RI imposes a 5% tax on whole-home short-term rentals, distinct from and not stacked with the existing 5% state hotel tax on single-room rentals. The local hotel tax rose from 1% to 2% the same day, bringing the total possible STR tax load to roughly 14%. Tax applies by date of occupancy, not date of booking. Sources: RI Division of Taxation notice, RI Realtors.
  • Jamestown RI. Local registration after state DBR registration, via an OpenGov portal. $350 annual fee, inspection by building official and fire marshal, re-inspection every 3 years and 2 years respectively. Registration opens the prior fall; the process takes 4–6 weeks. Source: Jamestown Press.
  • North Kingstown RI. No dedicated townwide STR ordinance or public registry page found. The town appears to manage STR use through general zoning, building and assessor review — note the homestead tax exemption can be affected by short-term leasing. All RI hosts must still register with the state Department of Business Regulation if listing on a third-party platform. Source: RI DBR.

Duplex zoning notes

  • Narragansett RI — new this run. Town officials are reported to have an active proposal to ban duplexes across most of the town, allowing them only by special permit in two zones, in response to Rhode Island's 2023 housing-production legislation. Existing ordinance already demands very large minimum lots for a duplex — 100,000 sqft buildable area in R80 and 60,000 sqft in R-40. This is a live regulatory risk against any Narragansett duplex purchase (194 S Pier Rd and 6 MacAlder St above) and should be verified with the town planning department before an offer.
  • South Kingstown RI. Duplexes are allowed by right in R10, R20, RM, MU, CN, CD and CW. R40, R80 and R200 are single-family only; R30 requires a special use permit. An accessory apartment cannot be added on top of an already-permitted two-household dwelling unless it replaces an existing unit. Zone matters enormously here — confirm the parcel's district before assuming a conversion or rebuild is allowed.
  • North Kingstown RI. Two-family dwellings are permitted in the Neighborhood Residential (NR) district per Article III. Other districts were not fully enumerated in retrievable sources; verify parcel-specific zoning with planning (401-268-1571).
  • Warwick RI. Ordinance language allows two-family and multiple-family developments and even permits more than one residential structure per lot in specific conformity sections (308, 309, 502), but the district-by-district permitted-use table was not fully retrievable this run. Warwick is generally the most permissive of these towns on multi-family, reflected in its larger inventory — but confirm the district for the specific shore parcel. Note this interacts with the R-6/R-10 STR bar above: a Warwick duplex may be buildable and rentable long-term while still barred from non-owner-occupied short-term rental.
  • Barrington RI. The town maintains a zoning use table and dimensional code (Chapter 185), but the specific two-family district permissions were not retrievable. Barrington is a low-density, largely single-family suburb; existing two-families there are commonly legal-nonconforming rather than newly permitted uses. Confirm with Barrington's Building and Zoning Office before assuming any new conversion is allowed.
  • Massachusetts corridor towns. Every MA duplex found is an existing legal non-conforming or grandfathered two-family use in a residential zone; none of the listings state new multi-family construction rights. Attleboro MA's downtown-adjacent neighbourhoods (Hope St, the Mendon Rd corridor, S Main St) hold the deepest two-family stock. A buyer should verify with each town's building department whether a purchased two-family retains its non-conforming status if substantially rebuilt.
  • Jamestown RI, Warren RI. No district-level detail retrieved this run. General results indicate Jamestown's ordinance does include provisions for detached one- and two-family dwellings. Treat all zoning statements above as a starting point for due diligence, not a substitute for a parcel-level title and zoning search.

Coverage & caveats

  • Source gating was non-uniform across agents again this run, and in the opposite direction from last week. Zillow and Redfin were fully open on the northern-corridor sweep (Canton/Stoughton/Sharon/Norwood MA) and on the RI duplex sweep, but Zillow was JS-gated on the southern-corridor sweep (Mansfield/Attleboro/Rehoboth MA), where only search snippets were readable and no detail page could be fetched. Coverage depth is therefore uneven between town clusters and the two clusters are not strictly comparable on inventory counts.
  • LandWatch and Land.com were JS-gated for every town-filtered search on the northern corridor — requests returned "0 Properties / Javascript must be enabled" and fell back to unrelated statewide inventory (Berkshire, Worcester, Franklin County). Snippet-level counts from those sites could not be independently confirmed and were deliberately excluded from the tables. This is a real gap, likely single digits of additional land parcels per northern town. The same sites worked on the southern sweep.
  • Homes.com was the single richest and most reliable source this run across all three sections. Compass, LandSearch, Houzeo, Raveis, Boardwalk Properties/Boston Pads and the RI Statewide MLS IDX feed (searchrirealestate.com) were all fully accessible.
  • The Blackstone Team (the-blackstoneteam.com) was JS-gated on its Oakland Beach RI multi-family page — navigation chrome only, no server-rendered listing data. Impact was minor: Homes.com independently supplied the Oakland Beach listing (19 Wood St, Warwick RI).
  • Realtor.com, Movoto, Trulia, Point2Homes, Mott & Chace, Lila Delman (except one exclusion-confirming listing), Residential Properties Ltd and RE/MAX RI appeared only as search snippets and were not fetched directly. In this market they largely mirror the same MLS PIN and RI Statewide feeds already covered, so incremental unique inventory is likely small — but broker-exclusive and pocket listings on those platforms cannot be ruled out.
  • Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist and other FSBO channels were not checked. Non-MLS inventory should be treated as uncovered.
  • Several primary legal sources were unreachable: the Warwick RI ordinance PDF and Municode zoning text (blank on fetch), policycommons.net (Warwick zoning amendment document), and the South Kingstown RI rental-registration page itself (blank on direct fetch — the ordinance details above come from the town's search summary plus The Independent).
