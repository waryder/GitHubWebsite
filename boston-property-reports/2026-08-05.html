Short-term-rental rules by town

  • Narragansett RI — highest uncertainty, still unresolved. The 2024 ordinance (7-night minimum plus a declining permit cap, 1,100 → 1,000 → 900 by September 2026) has been under court injunction since 2024-10-02 (Judge Sarah Taft-Carter, RI Superior Court, Case No. WC-2024-0372, Narragansett 2100, Inc. v. Town of Narragansett) and remains unenforced. The most recent dated reporting located is the Narragansett Times of 2025-12-19, describing Town Council discussion of scrapping the ordinance and rewriting STR rules as a zoning ordinance instead — no vote taken, and no 2026 reporting confirming resolution was found this run. Verify the current docket status with the Town Solicitor and RI Superior Court directly before any Narragansett RI purchase.
  • Statewide RI 5% whole-home STR tax — confirmed in force. Effective 2026-01-01 per RI Division of Taxation Advisory ADV 2025-16, applying to any dwelling rented in its entirety short-term through any channel including Airbnb and VRBO. The local hotel tax also doubled from 1% to 2%, putting combined tax on short stays at roughly 14%. No rollback found as of 2026-08-05.
  • North Kingstown RI: registration ordinance (Ord. 23-19, Art. XVIII) requires Town Clerk registration for units let under 30 days. No permit cap and no owner-occupancy requirement found.
  • South Kingstown RI (Wakefield RI, Peace Dale RI, Matunuck RI): rental registry adopted April 2024 (Town Code §§9-200–9-210), $300 per unit for stays of 30 days or less, deadline extended to 2026-03-31. No cap found.
  • Jamestown RI: registration required since August 2023, $350/yr fee, occupancy cap of two per bedroom plus two, and a local point of contact who can respond within two hours. No numeric permit cap.
  • Warwick RI: STR ordinance since February 2023 — $250/yr registration, owner or agent must live within 50 miles, occupancy cap of two per bedroom. No cap on total permits found. Note: an earlier run of this report flagged a possible citywide zoning restriction on non-owner-occupied short-term rentals in Warwick RI; that restriction was not corroborated in this run's sources. Given how much of Section 2 sits in Warwick RI, confirm the current zoning position with the City directly before committing.

Roughly 20–40 listings per corridor town sit behind the Redfin wall unverified, and an estimated 10–20% of active RI beach-town inventory (newer, exclusive or FSBO listings not yet syndicated to Homes.com) is unconfirmed. Because these aggregators largely share the same MLS feed — MLSPIN in Massachusetts, State-Wide MLS in Rhode Island — the hidden inventory probably overlaps heavily with what is shown rather than representing unique supply, but that cannot be verified.
  • Most productive sources were LandSearch, LandWatch, Land.com, Homes.com, Compass and Movoto for Massachusetts, and Homes.com plus first-party association and town websites for Rhode Island.
  • Utilities data is thin. Only three parcels in the entire corridor set explicitly confirm public water at the street — Oak St, Attleboro MA (water and sewer both, stubbed to the lot), 10 Cobbler Ln, Sharon MA and 190 N Main St, Sharon MA, plus 814–816 Neponset St, Norwood MA for water only.   • Stale aggregator statuses. Several listings were excluded because cached aggregator pages showed “active” or “pending” on parcels independently confirmed sold — 230 Richland Rd, Norwood MA; 92 Union St, Norwood MA; Lot 2 Pondview Ln, Stoughton MA; 75 Fowler St, Mansfield MA.
